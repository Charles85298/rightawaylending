Step-by-step homebuyer guide

From your first questions to closing day.

A practical overview of the mortgage and homebuying process. Actual requirements, timing, costs, and eligibility vary by borrower, property, program, and lender.

1. Start with a comfortable budget

Begin with the monthly payment you can reasonably manage—not only the maximum purchase price. A total housing payment can include principal, interest, property taxes, homeowners insurance, mortgage insurance, association dues, and other costs.

2. Review credit, income, assets, and debts

Mortgage professionals commonly review your credit history, monthly obligations, employment and income, available funds, and the type of property being financed. These details help determine which programs may be considered.

3. Discuss pre-qualification and pre-approval

The terms are sometimes used differently. A more complete review generally involves verifying more financial information. Neither term should be treated as a guarantee of final loan approval.

4. Compare loan options

Conventional, FHA, VA, USDA, jumbo, and other programs have different features and eligibility considerations. The right comparison includes payment, cash needed, mortgage insurance, fees, and longer-term goals.

5. Prepare documents

Common requests may include identification, pay stubs, W-2s or tax returns, bank statements, proof of funds, and information about current debts. Self-employed borrowers and investors may need additional documentation.

6. Shop for a home

Coordinate with a qualified real-estate professional and stay in contact with your mortgage team. Avoid major credit, employment, banking, or debt changes without first understanding how they may affect the transaction.

7. Application, processing, and underwriting

After a property is selected, the file may move through disclosures, document collection, appraisal, title work, processing, and underwriting. The underwriter may request additional information or conditions before issuing a final decision.

8. Review closing information

Before closing, carefully review the final terms, cash needed, payment details, and settlement documents. Ask questions about anything that is unclear.

Important: This guide is educational and does not represent an approval, commitment to lend, legal advice, tax advice, or a complete description of any loan program.
