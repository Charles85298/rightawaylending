Section 1: Business Overview
1. What does Right Away Lending do?
Operates as an independent, third-party intermediary that matches homebuyers with wholesale lenders. Unlike direct lenders or retail banks, a broker company does not use its own money to fund loans and does not make underwriting decisions. Instead, they act as an outsourced sales and processing team, shopping a borrower’s file across dozens of different financial institutions to find the best interest rate and loan terms.
The 4 Step Broker Workflow
• Sourcing and Intake: The broker meets with the borrower, analyzes their credit, verifies their income, and packages their financial documents into a single loan file. 
• Wholesale Shopping: The broker inputs the file into an automated pricing engine to compare wholesale rates from numerous backend lenders simultaneously.
• Lender Submission: Once the client selects a loan program, the broker submits the file to that specific wholesale lender’s underwriting department.
• Pipeline Processing: The broker’s internal processing team acts as a buffer, collecting missing documents from the borrower and pushing the lender to clear conditions for closing. 
• Key Advantages of Using a Broker
• Access to Wholesale Pricing: Brokers get volume-discounted interest rates from wholesale lenders that everyday consumers cannot access directly. 
• One Application, Multiple Options: A borrower only needs to pull their credit score and submit paystubs once to compare programs across multiple banks.
• Niche Product Availability: Brokers excel at matching non-traditional borrowers (like self-employed individuals or investors) with specialized products like bank statement loans or DSCR loans.
• Lower Overhead Costs: Because broker shops don't maintain massive corporate real estate or complex capital requirements, they can often pass operational savings down via lower fees.

15. Common questions
Financial and Cost Questions
• What will my exact monthly mortgage payment be? Borrowers want to know their total out-of-pocket monthly cost, including principal, interest, property taxes, home insurance, and private mortgage insurance (PMI).
• How much money do I need for a down payment? Buyers frequently ask if they truly need a 20% down payment or if they qualify for low-down-payment programs like FHA (3.5%) or Conventional (3%).
• What are my estimated closing costs? Borrowers need to know what lender fees, title fees, and escrow pre-paids will cost them at the closing table, which usually totals 2% to 5% of the loan amount.
• Should I pay points to lower my interest rate? Clients want to know if buying discount points upfront makes long-term financial sense based on how long they plan to keep the home. 
Qualification and Process Questions
• What credit score do I need to get approved? Applicants want to know the minimum credit score required for different loan types and how their score impacts their interest rate.
• How much home can I actually afford? Borrowers ask this to understand how lenders calculate their debt-to-income (DTI) ratio and to find their maximum purchase price comfort zone.
• What documents do I need to provide? Buyers need a checklist of required items, usually encompassing W-2s, tax returns, pay stubs, and bank statements. 
• How long does the mortgage approval process take? Borrowers ask this to coordinate their moving timelines and ensure they can meet the closing date written into their real estate contract.
Strategy and Product Questions
• Should I choose a fixed-rate or an adjustable-rate mortgage (ARM)? Borrowers weigh the long-term stability of a 30-year fixed loan against the short-term savings of an ARM.
• What is the difference between pre-qualification and pre-approval? Buyers ask this to ensure they have a verified, heavyweight pre-approval letter before they start making offers on homes

23. Words to avoid
Regulatory and Compliance Red Flags
• "Guaranteed"
• Underwriting rules change constantly. Never promise an approval before a underwriter signs off.
• "Rates are going up tomorrow"
• Creating fake urgency violates the Truth in Lending Act (TILA) advertising rules.
• "You should move to this neighborhood"
• Steering buyers toward specific areas triggers federal Fair Housing Act violations.
• "Don't worry about that fee"
• Downplaying costs violates RESPA rules regarding accurate Loan Estimates.
Deceptive or Aggressive Sales Language
• "No-money-down loan"
• Almost all zero-down loans (like VA or USDA) still require closing costs. [1, 2]
• "This is a free refinance"
• Refinances are never free. The closing costs are simply rolled into the loan balance. [1]
• "Trust me, you can afford this"
• Let the debt-to-income (DTI) math speak for itself instead of pushing your opinion. [1]
• "Just sign here, we will fix the paperwork later"
• This sounds predatory and destroys consumer trust immediately.
Confusing Industry Jargon
• "Your DTI is too high"
• Say "debt-to-income ratio" and explain how monthly bills compare to gross income. [1]
• "We need to check the AUS"
• Say "automated underwriting system" so the borrower knows a computer is processing their data.
• "The P&I is set, but the TI might change"
• Say "Principal and Interest" and "Taxes and Insurance" separately to avoid confusing them.
